Why Krispy Kreme

A box for every sign-off

At Shake Shack, every promotion has to be earned through a sign-off. You run a real shift alone, with no help from other managers, exactly as you would in the new role. Quality, compliance, every touchpoint and every guest interaction gets checked.

The ladder is long: Team Member, Multi-Station, Cross Trainer, Assistant Manager, Assistant General Manager, then General Manager.

My first sign-off memory belongs to someone else. I was still in training at Canary Wharf when one of my managers passed his. Once he had signed off, we bought Krispy Kreme doughnuts for the whole team, all 35 to 38 of us.

When my own sign-off came, I did exactly the same for the team that had trained me, to say thank you.

After that, I made it a rule.

Whenever anyone passed a sign-off, at any level, the whole team got doughnuts. A new Multi-Station and a new General Manager got the same box.

The ritual came with me to Birmingham, where we opened a new restaurant with a new team. New faces, same box.

Those boxes came to mean much more than doughnuts. The whole team, from new starters to managers, stopped to celebrate one person's step up. People who hadn't signed off yet started asking when their box would come, and worked towards it. On a site as busy as Birmingham, it made us feel like a family. And every box took me back to my own sign-off day at Canary Wharf.

For my last year at Shake Shack, Krispy Kreme marked every promotion on my teams. Now I'd like to help open its stores in Ahmedabad, and build the next team that earns those boxes.

How a city opens

From weeks before launch to the daily count

Seven stages, as a new market meets them. Each story is real, from Shake Shack, Fresh Bake or Greggs.

Weeks beforeShake Shack Birmingham

Rehearse the busy days before they happen

Birmingham was opened by a management team of four, and I was one of them. Before the doors opened we ran trial runs for busy periods, with trainees moving through every station hour by hour. We also gave food to our neighbours, and to some early guests, to build goodwill before the first real queue.

  • Supply was set up before opening day: weekly orders across 11 suppliers and about 180 lines, to each supplier's cut-off.
  • Every delivery checked against its purchase order, with shortages chased for credit.
Opening weeksBirmingham, Dec 2024

Fifteen days of queues

I hadn't realised how much Birmingham would love Shake Shack. There were queues every day for 15 days, with 35 to 40 people on shift, most of them brand-new hires, and huge deliveries arriving on top. A new site can get an unannounced food-hygiene inspection at any time, so compliance had to be ready alongside the guest experience from the first hour. It was the biggest challenge of my hospitality career.

  • Built the Birmingham management and team rosters from their first version.
  • Set the daily and weekly sales forecasts and planned labour, prep and ordering to them.
Every shiftStandards

Walk the store before the guest does

The travel path is a fixed walk past 13 checkpoints, from outside the front door through the kitchen, chillers and storage to the staff room. At Birmingham it was walked every 90 minutes to two hours, all day, so an empty station or a fridge running warm showed up while it was still cheap to fix.

  • Full operations audit in my last week at Birmingham: 99.9%, with front-of-house at 100% and the inspector's note "A near perfectly managed Shack!"
  • The NSF food-safety auditor singled out handwashing, cross-contamination control and temperature control as best practice on site. NSF is an independent food-safety auditor.
Every morningForecast

Find the problem at 11:00, not at 18:00

After a huge trading day, the next delivery arrived short. The 11:00 forecast showed beef patties running out by about 18:00 and buns an hour later. Friends at other Shake Shack sites sent buns, and one came in person to help. Nobody could deliver beef, so another manager came in early to run the floor while I drove about an hour and a half each way to collect patties from another store. Back before 18:00, with 15 patties left, we traded normally until the 22:00 close.

Every bakeGreggs, Birmingham

Bake to the sales pattern

Greggs is a bakery, and the store I ran had been baking to a system that made more than it sold. We changed three things. Four of each bestselling sandwich went on display first thing, with more made after midday only if sales called for it. Savouries were baked in small batches, with a new tray going in when two were left. Ordering was reset to real sales, with weekdays planned against quieter weekends.

Sales went up and waste came down, with no extra people. A doughnut brand sells on freshness too, so this is the habit I'd bring to every Ahmedabad store.

Every countCost and suppliers

Read the number before reacting to it

One August 2025 stock count at Birmingham showed stock about £4,000 below where it should have been. Comparing it with the previous period, when the stock system had been down and the count had swung the other way, showed a real gap of under £40 across the two. Chicken bites was the one line still worth investigating.

At Fresh Bake I thought the uniform quote could be beaten. After my shifts I researched local suppliers in Leicester and negotiated until one accepted my price, then did the same for ingredients. Only once the saving was significant did I take it to the Operations Director, who checked it himself and switched suppliers.

  • Wrote the cost-of-goods report twice a month and reviewed the site P&L every period.
  • Challenged delivery-app refunds line by line and recovered 70% of what Deliveroo refunded from May to July 2025.
Every guestPeople

Turn one fix into something the team runs

At Fresh Bake, a couple who could neither hear nor speak, both allergic to dairy, were struggling to order. I gave them pen and paper, then showed them each ingredient one by one: an iced latte with oat milk and an iced mocha with almond milk. After their second visit their order went on a note at the counter and the team was briefed, so anyone on shift could serve them. On my last day they cried and took a photo with me.

At Birmingham, one colleague was struggling with the pace and I came close to letting him go in his probation. We talked after a shift as friends, and he told me about a condition I hadn't known about. He showed me how to hand him tasks in a way that worked for him. Two months later he was Employee of the Month.

Six habits

What I do in every store

New tasks

Do it first, then time it

Before handing a task over, I do it myself and time it. Then the team gets the task, a time limit and proper coaching.

Every 90 minutes

Walk the floor

The travel path catches a warm fridge or an empty station while it is still a small fix.

Start of day

Forecast early

A shortage found in the morning can still be solved. One found at the evening peak reaches the guest.

Hiring

Energy and attitude first

A smiling face and the right attitude can't be taught. Everything else can be trained on shift.

After shift

Talk as a friend first

After a hard shift, I talk to people as a friend before talking as a manager. That is how team members came to bring their problems to me.

Ownership

Every corner is mine

No "that's the owner's problem" and no "not in my job description". What counts is the result, however long it takes.

What will I bring with me?

Two working cultures. From India, jugaad: when a plan breaks, I find the fastest workable fix and stay until the job is done, however many hours it takes. From the UK, the standards that make a brand trustworthy in every store: food safety, allergen control, audits and a set routine for every shift. The fix can be creative, but it still has to pass the same food-safety and brand checks.

  • Openings: trial runs, station-by-station training and supply set up before the doors open.
  • Standards: the travel path, audit readiness every day and allergen documentation behind every product.
  • Costs: forecasting, cost-of-goods reports, stock variances, supplier pricing and the period P&L review.
  • People: hiring for energy and attitude, cross-training so most people can work most stations, and a sign-off culture people want to climb.
  • Delivery: three delivery apps in the UK and the refund disputes that come with them.
